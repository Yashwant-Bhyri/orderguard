# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Builders and operators of AI trading agents, plus the people who set and review the agents' risk mandates. They need to see what the agent proposed, what OrderGuard trusted, which checks decided the outcome, and what evidence supports that decision.

## Product Purpose

OrderGuard places a locally controlled, human-authorized boundary around simulated orders from AI agents. It checks untrusted instructions and claims, validates orders against signed limits and trusted prices, evaluates independent-source triggers, and records paper-receiver outcomes. Success means unsafe simulated actions are contained, permitted actions remain usable, and each decision can be understood and audited. The product is a research prototype; it does not place real trades or claim production broker readiness.

## Brand Personality

Precise, calm, candid. The interface should make consequential decisions legible, explain holds and refusals in plain language, and show uncertainty beside the evidence it qualifies.

## Anti-references

- Interfaces that imply a simulated order was a real fill or that a research prototype guarantees safety.
- Opaque risk scores, unexplained verdicts, or agent-provided numbers presented as trusted facts.
- Decorative trading dashboards that bury the decision in generic charts, badges, or AI-themed visual effects.

## Design Principles

1. Make the allow, hold, refuse, or quarantine decision and its reasons immediately clear.
2. Distinguish agent claims from independently trusted values wherever they affect a decision.
3. Show the checks and evidence behind each outcome so a reviewer can trace it.
4. Keep human authorization and bounded simulation explicit throughout the workflow.
5. State limitations and uncertainty where the related result appears.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA. Support keyboard operation, visible focus, semantic landmarks and labels, reduced motion, and verdict communication that does not rely on color alone. Preserve readable layouts at narrow viewports and when text is enlarged.
