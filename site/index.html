    The problem

    Six questions agent trading can't answer yet

    Every desk putting AI agents near money will be asked these. Today most stacks answer them with a prompt. OrderGuard answers them in code, with a model trained for the job, and with a record you can replay.

    1. A thousand agents read the same market. Does any of them know what the other nine hundred and ninety-nine just did?

      They share one signed budget, reserved atomically before any order commits, so when one agent spends risk every other agent has less room that same instant. A risk model trained on swarms scores every order against what the whole swarm is doing.

      of bad orders fit every per-order limit in the recorded run. got through.

    2. Whose price is the agent using?

      Its own, usually, and agents hallucinate prices and get fed forged ones. OrderGuard sizes every order at a trusted market price, never the one in the order, so a fooled agent's arithmetic can't move money.

      A forged split notice produced an order at the cap. Refused in code.

    3. Did that headline really happen?

      Agents pull from the open web. Text is screened before they read it, figures an order cites are checked against SEC filings, and an order that acts on news needs two independent sources first.

      orders acted on unconfirmed news, worth . All held.

    4. Is the agent doing what it said it would?

      Agents don't know when they've drifted. A calibrated decision model reads the agent's stated intent next to the order it produced and holds the ones that don't match. It returns probabilities, not prose.

      of mismatched orders held, of matching ones.

    5. Who checks the mandate before someone signs it?

      People mistype limits, and agents drafting mandates do worse. Code proves a draft fits inside the signed parent. The decision model flags drafts that are too broad or wrong for their purpose, and a forecaster shows what the draft would let through before anyone signs.

      of bad drafts flagged on held-out cases.

    6. What happens when the market moves against all of them at once?

      Agents pile into the same names and their own fills move the price. OrderGuard was run inside an impact-aware market where shocks spread across symbols, with every order still decided by the real authority.

      Eight layers. Only code says yes

      Models read text, score behaviour and rank the queue. Whether an order reaches the ledger is decided by a signed mandate, a trusted price and plain code, in that order.

      1. The owner signs a mandate

        Symbols, sides, per-order and daily caps, a price band and a sign-off size, signed with the owner's key. Change one character and every order is refused.

      2. Prices come from venues, not agents

        Every order is sized at a trusted market price, the median across venues, never at the price the agent claims.

      3. The mandate check decides

        Allow, hold or refuse in microseconds, each with reasons in plain words. Shared budgets and portfolio caps stop many agents from splitting one big position.

      4. The risk model can only tighten

        It scores every allowed order against the whole swarm. It can turn an allow into a hold or a refusal, never the other way; a property test checks that over thousands of random decisions.

      5. People review what matters first

        Held orders queue by expected bad dollars, with the risk model's reasons and the decision model's estimate attached. There is no code path from advice to an approval.

      6. The receiver writes the ledger

        The final authority re-checks the signature at commit time, with idempotency, revocation and a hash-chained ledger anyone can replay.

      Before any of this, what agents read is screened. Hidden characters and look-alike tickers are exposed in code (L1), manipulative text is caught and its repeats quarantined (L2, L3), cited figures are checked against SEC filings (L6), and a "sell if it crashes" rule fires only when two independent sources agree (L7).

      Let the agent be wrong. Only the right orders come out

      OrderGuard is the cushion between any agent and any venue. Plug in a research agent, a chat assistant or a swarm over MCP or HTTP. Let it hallucinate, read poisoned pages and get talked into things. What comes out the other side is grounded, inside the signed mandate and on the record; everything else is held with a reason a person can act on.
