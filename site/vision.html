Where OrderGuard is going · in design

Permitted actions keep counting

A payment ends at checkout. A trade becomes a position that keeps moving, interacts with everything else you hold, and adds to tomorrow's risk. The economic consequences of permitted actions accumulate over time, so authorizing each order is not enough. Authority has to be enforced against the portfolio as it will be.

The failure nobody broke a rule for

Twenty agents, every order within its limit

Each agent has a sensible reason for its trade, and every order is under its cap. Together they put most of the portfolio on one theme. Then the theme falls.

What changes when the trader is an agent

Existing controls ask about the order. Agents raise questions about the decision

Brokers and trading platforms already check size, positions, prices and message rates, and they can stop an algorithm. We build on that. The questions on the right are the ones agents add.

  1. Is the order too large?Does the order still serve the investment purpose it was delegated for?
  2. Is the account over its position limit?Are agents independently reinforcing one mistaken thesis?
  3. Is the price outside permitted bounds?Was the decision based on fabricated or stale information?
  4. Has an algorithm exceeded its message rate?Has an agent entered a repetitive reasoning or action loop?
  5. Can a desk stop the algorithm?Can authority be withdrawn across spawned agents and pending actions?
  6. Can the firm reconstruct an execution?Can it reconstruct the evidence, the delegated authority, the policy and the action that followed?

The Autonomy Contract

A delegation contract enforced against the portfolio and everything still outstanding

Not a bigger rulebook. Four parts that work as one loop.

  1. Signed specificationWhat the owner actually signed, kept apart from what they said and what a model proposed
  2. Authoritative portfolio statePositions, working orders and reserved risk, as they will be if everything fills
  3. Admission controllerAllow, hold or refuse. Signed limits decide; models can only make it stricter
  4. Continuous feedback loopObserve, evaluate, control, reconcile, and observe again

Part one · Signed specification

What you said, what a model proposed, and what you signed are three different things

A model can turn "don't bet everything on one theme" into numbers. It should never decide what those words mean on your behalf. It proposes, shows you the consequences, and only your signature makes the numbers binding.

Part two · Authoritative portfolio state

      
Three agents each check the position. Each sees room. Together there isn't any

      
Checked one at a time against today's position, three orders all look fine. Checked against the position as it will be, with every earlier order's capacity already reserved, the third one is caught.

      Part three · Admission against portfolio risk
      
      
      The same order, two portfolios, two different answers
      
      
      The question is not whether the order is too big. It is how the order changes the risk of what you already hold.
      
    
      
    
      
    
      
    
      Hard limits refuse; estimates like this one can only hold or tighten. Covariances shift in a crisis and tail estimates can be wrong, so statistical risk never overrides a signed limit, and losses under declared stress scenarios are kept as a separate, exact check.
      
  
      

      



      
  
      
    
      
      
      Part four · Continuous feedback loop
      
      
      An agent is a process, not a request
      
      
      It reads, revises, spawns sub-agents and keeps acting. So authority is leased, not granted forever: it expires unless renewed against the current state, and one revocation reaches every agent spawned under it.
      
    
      
    
  
      

      



      
  
      
    
      
      
      Every order carries its proof
      
      
      Not just "buy twenty shares"
      
      
      Each order arrives with a certificate: the contract and policy version it acts under, the portfolio snapshot it was checked against, the reservation it holds and the evidence it relied on. The receiver checks each line and seals a receipt.
      
      
      It proves what was checked and what evidence was presented. It does not prove the trade was wise, and we never claim it does.
      
    
      
    
  
      

      



      
  
      
    
      
      
      Research proposal
      
      
      Judgement that lasts longer than one order
      
      
      We propose a new kind of model for financial agents: one that holds a persistent understanding of what a book is for, which theses it is acting on and how fresh their evidence is, and judges each new action against that over weeks, not seconds. Trained on simulated markets where the long-run outcome is known. Bound by the same rule as every model in OrderGuard: it can make a decision stricter, never approve one.

      What we promise, and how we will prove it
      
      
      We don't make agents good traders. We make their authority bounded, supervisable and accountable
      
      
      The benchmark that matters is not OrderGuard against an unprotected agent. It is OrderGuard against a competent conventional risk stack, on failures that stack allows.
