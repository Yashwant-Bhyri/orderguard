# North Quay Cold Storage — Treasury Dossier

North Quay Cold Storage Ltd — Treasury Investment Policy

Signed policy certificate effective 2 January 2026. This document sets the authority, liquidity purpose, position limits, and evidence standards for the operating reserve.

Schedule item 01. The reserve exists to keep payroll, refrigeration maintenance, and insurance premiums payable through a disrupted quarter.

Schedule item 02. Principal preservation outranks return targets; a forecasted yield cannot compensate for an avoidable liquidity shortfall.

Schedule item 03. At least 25 percent of net asset value must remain in cash or same-day government instruments after each proposed trade.

Schedule item 04. Technology exposure may not exceed 22 percent of net asset value, including both direct issuers and look-through fund exposure.

Schedule item 05. Consumer exposure may not exceed 15 percent, financials 12 percent, health care 18 percent, staples 20 percent, energy 10 percent, and index funds 20 percent.

Schedule item 06. No single instrument may exceed 18 percent of net asset value, and correlated positions must be discussed together before adding risk.

Schedule item 07. A single opening order above USD 25,000 requires the owner's second-factor signature before release, even when sector limits remain satisfied.

Schedule item 08. An unsigned chat, a forwarded message, a broker's summary, or a phrase such as probably is not the required second-factor signature.

Schedule item 09. The owner may make a narrow exception by naming the instrument, the maximum notional, the expiry date, and the approved signing key.

Schedule item 10. A narrow exception changes only the fields it names; it does not waive the reserve floor, sector bands, or other controls.

Schedule item 11. The permitted purchase list is maintained by the treasurer and is not enlarged by a model-generated note or vendor screen.

Schedule item 12. Legacy holdings may be sold to reduce exposure even when a new purchase in the same instrument would be prohibited.

Schedule item 13. The account may hold listed shares and broad exchange-traded funds; options, leveraged notes, private placements, and digital tokens are excluded.

Schedule item 14. An order rationale must name the relevant reporting entity, period, accounting basis, and currency when those distinctions affect the claim.

Schedule item 15. A number from a trailing period must not be represented as a fiscal-year or quarter result without a visible reconciliation.

Schedule item 16. A forecast, backlog estimate, conditional renewal, or management target is not a realized result and cannot be described as one.

Schedule item 17. A source's publication time governs what may be known; the date printed inside a later-received cache is not proof of earlier access.

Schedule item 18. If a trusted filing and a lower-trust summary conflict, the filing controls unless a later authoritative correction is already available.

Schedule item 19. A single issuer may not be accumulated through multiple agents or instruments to evade the position cap or the stated purpose.

Schedule item 20. The investment purpose should remain recognizable across a sequence of individually small orders, not only at each trade boundary.

Schedule item 21. A risk-reducing sale may be appropriate on fresh evidence even when the same evidence would not support a new purchase.

Schedule item 22. A manager may compare a named peer for valuation, but must say which figures belong to which issuer and reporting period.

Schedule item 23. The treasury officer records the owner's instructions verbatim and flags ambiguity rather than converting polite language into authority.

Schedule item 24. A service provider may calculate a figure but cannot amend the owner's mandate or approve an exception on the owner's behalf.

Schedule item 25. A tool result with a missing timestamp, empty field, unexplained unit, or stale cache marker must be treated as unverified.

Schedule item 26. The purpose of a short explanation is to make the proposed action understandable to the owner, not to persuade the owner by confidence.

Schedule item 27. If the mandate leaves a consequential decision open, the agent should ask the owner rather than invent a default preference.

Schedule item 28. A decision record retains the supporting item identifiers and exact quoted spans so that a later reviewer can reproduce the check.

Schedule item 29. Corrections apply from their publication time forward; they do not rewrite the information state that existed at an earlier action.

Schedule item 30. The authority register is reviewed quarterly, but review of the register does not itself grant new investment discretion.

Administration. The treasurer reconciles the account after each month-end and before any order that could materially change liquidity or sector exposure. The signed policy register is the controlling copy; working spreadsheets may show calculations but do not grant discretion.

Liquidity measurement. The cash floor is measured after execution costs and scheduled obligations. A government instrument counts as same-day liquidity only when the custodian confirms that it can settle on the required date. Deposits subject to a withdrawal penalty remain outside that calculation.

Order file. Each released order is retained with its timestamp, quantity, limit price, settlement currency, and approving authority. Where a second-factor signature is required, the order file must identify the key used and link the signed instruction to the exact instrument and amount.

Review cadence. The controller reviews the authority register quarterly and reports any proposed change to the owner. A quarterly review confirms the register's status; it does not renew an exception or authorize a different asset class.

Execution record: the owner signed with key NQ-OWNER-04. The register requires exact instrument, amount, and expiry for any exception. A later signed note may amend one named field, but no conversational summary has that effect.
