# How investment purpose, mandates and theses are represented, monitored and judged

Scope: how asset management (US and EU) writes down "what this money is for", checks trades against it (by machines and by people), detects drift, documents theses, applies formal "serves the client" standards, elicits preferences, and how much experts agree. Written for OrderGuard's System One, which needs a typed target for "does this agent action still serve the delegated purpose". Current as of November 2026. Research budget was limited: vendor documentation for Aladdin, Bloomberg AIM and SimCorp was not retrieved (see Gaps).

## 1. Investment Policy Statements, management agreements and guidelines: contents, structure, machine-checkability

### Takeaway
The professional standard for writing down purpose is the IPS, organised as **objectives (return, risk) plus constraints (liquidity, time horizon, tax, legal/regulatory, unique circumstances)**, often shortened to "RRTTLLU". Objectives are partly quantitative and partly narrative. Constraints split into hard numeric limits a compliance engine can encode and qualitative preferences that need human interpretation. Even "hard" limits carry interpretation risk once they are coded.

### Cited Findings
- CFA Institute defines the IPS as a written record of a client's return goals and risk tolerance over a relevant horizon, plus constraints: liquidity needs, tax considerations, regulatory requirements and unique circumstances. It captures the client's "needs, preferences, and circumstances" as objectives and constraints. — [CFA Institute, The Portfolio Management Process and the IPS (2018 refresher)](https://www.cfainstitute.org/membership/professional-development/refresher-readings/2018/the-portfolio-management-process-and-the-investment-policy-statement)
- The 2026 CFA refresher lists the constraint categories as liquidity requirements, time horizon, regulatory requirements, tax status and unique needs. — [CFA Institute, Basics of Portfolio Planning and Construction (2026)](https://www.cfainstitute.org/insights/professional-learning/refresher-readings/2026/basics-of-portfolio-planning-and-construction)
- Time horizon is "the time period associated with an investment objective", which may be short, long or a combination (multi-stage). Unique circumstances are internal factors beyond liquidity, horizon and tax that constrain portfolio choices. — [CFA Institute 2018 reading PDF](https://www.cfainstitute.org/-/media/documents/article/refresher-readings-free/rr-2018-l2v6r47.pdf)
- CFA Standard III(C) (Suitability) guidance: best practice is a written IPS covering time horizon, liquidity, tax and legal/regulatory factors. Some clients add unique constraints or preferences such as ESG factors. — [CFA Institute, Standard III(C) guidance](https://www.cfainstitute.org/en/ethics-standards/ethics/code-of-ethics-standards-of-conduct-guidance/standards-of-practice-III-C)
- Constraints interact: a longer horizon raises the ability to take risk and lowers liquidity needs. This is a study-aid summary of CFA curriculum. — [Ryan O'Connell, IPS & RRTTLLU guide](https://ryanoconnellfinance.com/investment-policy-statement/)
- CFA risk tolerance has two parts, **ability** and **willingness** to take risk. This comes from the same curriculum: Weber & Klement separate "perceived-risk tolerance" from "apparent-risk tolerance". — [CFA Institute Research Foundation, Risk Tolerance and Circumstances (2018)](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/foundation/2018/risk-tolerance-and-circumstances)
- Guideline coding is "the foundation of investment compliance". To monitor a client's requirements, the whole guideline has to be defined in a compliance engine. — [Acuity Knowledge Partners, guideline coding](https://www.acuitykp.com/guideline-coding-and-monitoring-challenges)
- Restrictions an engine cannot enforce automatically are called **non-codeable guidelines (NCGs)** and are monitored through separate procedures. — [Acuity KP, guideline monitoring challenges](https://acuitykp.com/explore-solution/guideline-monitoring-challenges)
- Coding involves interpretation even for numeric limits. Some managers read "minimum of 80% in equities" literally, while others set a minimum of 90% (an internal buffer) or 67%. — [Acuity KP, guideline coding challenges](https://www.acuitykp.com/blog/challenges-faced-in-coding-of-investment-restrictions) (that URL now redirects to acuityanalytics.com; the quote is from the search-result snippet)
- A regulatory example of a purpose label turned into a machine-checkable rule: the US Names Rule requires a fund whose name suggests a focus to adopt an 80% investment policy. Compliance dates for the 2023 amendments were pushed to 11 June 2026 (fund groups of $1bn or more) and 11 December 2026 (smaller groups). — [Katten, SEC extends Names Rule compliance dates](https://katten.com/insights/advisories/sec-extends-compliance-dates-for-the-names-rule-amendments). In February 2026 the SEC extended the Form N-PORT Names Rule reporting dates (to 17 November 2027 for groups of $10bn or more) and proposed removing those N-PORT requirements altogether. — [BDO, Changes to the Names Rule](https://arch.bdo.com/Changes-to-SEC-Names-Rule-Will-Affect-Registered-Funds). Sources conflict on which schedule is current; check against the SEC release.

### Inferences
- Taken together, the IPS skeleton is a usable type schema for an owner's mandate: `objective.return` (target or required, with horizon), `objective.risk` (ability vs willingness, often as max drawdown, volatility or loss tolerance), `constraints.{liquidity, horizon[stages], tax, legal, unique[]}`. Owner phrases like "don't bet everything on one theme" map to concentration constraints (unique or legal type). "Reduce overpriced tech" maps to a directional tilt objective with a valuation predicate. A tilt is neither a return objective nor a constraint, and the IPS has no dedicated slot for it (see section 4, theses).
- The 80%-equities example shows that a typed target needs both the literal threshold and the **interpretation policy** (buffer, numerator/denominator, look-through, measurement time), or two correct systems will disagree.
- A "names rule" view is useful for OrderGuard: if an agent is labelled with a purpose ("income agent", "tech-reduction agent"), a minimum share of exposure consistent with that label is the cheapest machine check of purpose fidelity.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve a primary investment management agreement template (e.g. an institutional IMA schedule of guidelines), so typical IMA section structure is not cited here.
- No source gave a measured share of real-world guidelines that are codable vs non-codable.

## 2. Guideline and compliance monitoring systems: pre-trade and post-trade rules, handling of soft guidelines

### Takeaway
Order management and compliance systems (Charles River, Aladdin, Bloomberg AIM, SimCorp) run coded rules at several points in the trade lifecycle: pre-trade, in-trade, post-trade, batch, what-if and historical. Qualitative guidelines are handled by a human-interpreted coding step, often vendor-assisted, plus separate manual monitoring of non-codeable guidelines. **The human judgement sits mostly at rule-authoring time and at exception review, not at each trade.**

### Cited Findings
- Charles River (CRD) runs compliance checks at pre-trade, in-trade, placement, post-trade, batch portfolio, as-of, what-if and historical stages. It offers a library of 2,000+ sample rules covering both regulatory and client-supplied rules. — [CRD, Compliance and Regulatory](https://www.crd.com/solutions/compliance/)
- CRD's Compliance Advisory Service has CRD compliance professionals work with each client to code and implement that client's existing rules into Charles River IMS. Interpretation is a consulting step, not an engine feature. — [CRD Compliance Advisory Service (PDF, 2026)](https://www.crd.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/CRD_Compliance-Advisory-Service.pdf)
- A vendor executive (2014): some rules are "cut and dried, others are a little gray", so the client decides on an interpretation before the vendor implements it. The vendor shares what it sees across its client base. — [Markets Media, Buy Side Assesses Pre-Trade Compliance](https://www.marketsmedia.com/buy-side-assesses-pre-trade-compliance) (dated, not CRD-specific)
- Recommended practice is to validate pre-trade, overnight/batch and trade compliance results through a validation process or what-if analysis. — [Acuity KP, guideline coding best practices](https://www.acuitykp.com/blog/guideline-coding-best-practices-monitoring)
- Non-codeable guidelines are monitored outside the engine through separate procedures. — [Acuity KP](https://acuitykp.com/explore-solution/guideline-monitoring-challenges)
- CFA Institute's Asset Manager Code is the related governance standard, which compliance teams use alongside guideline monitoring. — [AnalystPrep, Asset Manager Code](https://analystprep.com/study-notes/cfa-level-iii/the-asset-manager-code-of-conduct/)

### Inferences
- The industry design pattern is a **three-tier rule taxonomy**: (a) hard coded rules that block pre-trade (limits, prohibited lists, concentration); (b) soft or warning rules that trigger an override with a recorded justification; (c) non-codeable guidelines checked by periodic human review. OrderGuard's split between deterministic gates and a learned System One mirrors (a) and (b+c). The industry has **no automated judge for (c)**. That gap is System One's opportunity, and also where it has no precedent.
- "What-if" (simulate the portfolio after the trade, then evaluate the rules) is the standard way to judge a single trade against a portfolio-level purpose. System One should take the post-trade state as input, not just the order.
- Because interpretation is fixed when the rule is authored, ambiguous owner language ("overpriced tech") should be turned into typed predicates **at delegation time**, with the owner confirming, rather than reinterpreted on every trade.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve primary documentation on rule types or override workflows for BlackRock Aladdin Compliance, Bloomberg AIM or SimCorp Dimension/One (warning vs hard-block semantics, override justification fields). This was cut by the research budget, not because the documentation is unavailable.
- No public data on override rates, false-positive rates of pre-trade rules, or how long exception review takes.

## 3. Style drift: definition, detection, regulatory and consultant treatment, adaptation vs drift

### Takeaway
Style drift is a portfolio's divergence from its stated or benchmark style. It is detected from **holdings** (characteristics of what is held: more frequent and more precise, but needs data and arrives with a lag) or from **returns** (regressing returns on style indexes: cheap and high-frequency, but noisy). Mislabelling is common, around one-third of funds by some measures, and it is associated with weaker skill and underperformance.

### Cited Findings
- Holdings-based drift measures can follow drift in each style dimension as often as holdings are reported. They can separate drift from **active trades** from drift caused by **passively holding stocks whose characteristics change**. The authors argue these measures have advantages over returns-based drift measures. — [CFR Cologne working paper 12-04](https://www.cfr-cologne.de/download/workingpaper/cfr-12-04.pdf)
- Returns-based style analysis regresses fund returns on passive style indexes or constructed portfolios. Kaplan (Morningstar, 2003) compares the two methods and notes that holdings databases are expensive, so only a handful of research firms can do holdings-based analysis. — [Morningstar, Returns vs Holdings paper](https://gladmainnew.morningstar.com/directhelp/Returns_vs_HoldingsPaper.pdf)
- US mutual funds may report holdings up to 60 days after quarter-end, which is one reason returns-based methods are used for timeliness. — [Wikipedia, Style drift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Style_drift)
- Chua & Tam (2020, Journal of Corporate Finance): managers who engage in style drift show weaker stock-picking ability. — [Wikipedia, Style drift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Style_drift) (secondary)
- Of 1,866 US equity funds (2003–2015), about 14% were significantly misclassified, and misclassified funds underperformed by about 0.92% per year. — [Wikipedia, Style drift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Style_drift) (secondary; original study not verified)
- Sensoy (2009, JFE): almost **one-third** of active diversified US equity funds name a prospectus size/value-growth benchmark that does not match their actual style. Flows respond to performance against the mismatched benchmark, which suggests strategic mislabelling. — [Sensoy 2009, RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:92:y:2009:i:1:p:25-39)
- Chen, Evans & Sun (2022) find style-mismatched benchmarks covered 41% of fund assets (34% of funds) at the start of their sample, declining over time. Cremers, Fulkerson & Riley (2022) argue "corrected" benchmarks may still be inadequate. — as summarised in [AAII, EFA Award Paper 2023](https://www.aaii.com/files/images/EFA-Award-Paper-2023.pdf) and [Essex repository](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/34743/1/JFR-08-21-0892_Yan_BB_Author.pdf)
- Wermers, "A matter of style: the causes and consequences of style drift in institutional portfolios", examines why drift happens and what follows from it in institutional portfolios. — [EconBiz record](https://www.econbiz.de/Record/a-matter-of-style-the-causes-and-consequences-of-style-drift-in-institutional-portfolios-wermers-russ/10010957189)
- The regulatory treatment of purpose-label drift in the US is codified through the Names Rule 80% policy (see section 1). — [Katten](https://katten.com/insights/advisories/sec-extends-compliance-dates-for-the-names-rule-amendments)

### Inferences
- The holdings-based distinction between **active drift** (the agent traded away from purpose) and **passive drift** (market moves changed the characteristics of what is held) matters directly to OrderGuard. System One should attribute a purpose violation to the agent's *action* only when the action caused it. A separate supervision signal should handle passive drift, for example rebalancing duty versus a block.
- Legitimate adaptation vs drift: the sources suggest no formal test. In practice the line appears to be whether the shift is (a) within stated guideline ranges, (b) disclosed and explained in advance, and (c) consistent with the stated objective rather than chasing recent performance. This is inference, not a sourced standard.
- OrderGuard sees every action in real time, so it does not face the 60-day lag. Holdings-based purpose measures are cheap for it, and returns-based methods are mainly useful as a cross-check.

### Gaps
- I did not find an explicit consultant (e.g. Mercer, Cambridge) or regulator written standard separating "legitimate tactical adaptation" from drift.
- I did not verify the primary text of the 14% / 0.92% study, or diBartolomeo & Witkowski's (1997) misclassification estimate.

## 4. Investment theses: documentation, investment committees, pre-mortems, decision journals

### Takeaway
There is no regulatory or industry-standard thesis schema. Practitioner convention is consistent, though: **claim (falsifiable, causal) + catalysts (dated, hard vs soft) + horizon + invalidation / thesis-break criteria written before entry + position sizing + pre-mortem**. Committees value theses that are not rewritten after the fact. The key discipline is separating "price fell" from "thesis broke".

### Cited Findings
- Review triggers and invalidation criteria should be written **before purchase**, tied to facts and events rather than daily price moves. Pre-written criteria counter confirmation bias because the test was chosen before the outcome was known. — [thesis-tracker skill (practitioner)](https://tessl.io/registry/skills/github/HKUDS/Vibe-Trading/thesis-tracker); [Kubera, Investment thesis](https://www.kubera.com/blog/investment-thesis)
- "The price falling does not automatically break the thesis. The facts changing might." A pre-committed sell rule counters sunk-cost thinking and loss aversion. — [ai-berkshire thesis-tracker write-up](https://instagit.com/xbtlin/ai-berkshire/ai-berkshire-thesis-tracker-post-buying-discipline/)
- Practitioner note: setting threshold levels up front creates discipline, but executing the sell once a threshold breaks is the hard part, because ego protection makes the metrics seem less relevant. — [JB Global Fund, Metacognition](https://jbglobalfund.substack.com/p/metacognition)
- A memo template includes "Pre-Mortem: why could this investment fail?" and a "Thesis Break Condition: quantitative boundary that forces immediate exit". — [Pineify investment memo framework](https://pineify.app/investment-memo)
- Hypotheses can be structured as "If A then B" (causal, falsifiable) with "Unless C" (invalidation). Catalysts are split into hard (known dates) and soft (evolving narratives). — [claudskills, writing investment theses](https://claudskills.com/skills/writing-investment-theses/SKILL.md)
- Allocators and committees look for a consistent thesis structure across deals, evidence that theses are not rewritten after the fact, and clarity on downside scenarios. — [Altss, Investment thesis glossary](https://altss.com/glossary/investment-thesis)
- Klein's premortem (HBR, September 2007): the team assumes the project has already failed and generates plausible reasons why. Klein cites research that prospective hindsight improves the ability to identify reasons for future outcomes by 30%. — [Klein, Performing a Project Premortem (PDF copy)](https://wiki.duraspace.org/download/attachments/104563925/ProjectPremortem.pdf?api=v2). Caveat: the underlying study (Mitchell, Russo & Pennington 1989) measured the *number* of reasons generated, not their accuracy. — [Rework summary](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/project-management/pre-mortem)
- Source quality note: section 4 sources are mostly practitioner blogs and templates, not authoritative standards. Treat the section lists as convention.

### Inferences
- A typed **Thesis** object for an agent action could be: `claim` (if A then B), `mechanism`, `catalysts[{event, date|null, hard|soft}]`, `horizon`, `invalidation[{predicate on facts, not price}]`, `risk_budget/sizing`, `premortem_reasons[]`, `link_to_mandate` (which objective or constraint it serves). System One can then judge two separate things: (1) does the action fit the **mandate** (IPS-level), and (2) is it consistent with its **own thesis** (thesis-level, e.g. adding to a position whose invalidation predicate has fired).
- "Not rewritten after the fact" suggests OrderGuard should store theses immutably at decision time (a decision journal) and treat thesis edits that follow adverse outcomes as a risk signal.

### Gaps
- I found no published empirical evidence that written invalidation criteria or decision journals improve institutional performance, and no surveyed standard IC memo format from a regulator or a CFA body.

## 5. Suitability and best interest as a formal "serves the client's purpose" standard

### Takeaway
FINRA 2111, Reg BI and MiFID II suitability all turn "serves the client" into a **structured profile plus a reasonable-basis judgement**. None defines a pass/fail algorithm. Each specifies (a) the profile fields to collect, (b) separate tests at product level, customer level and pattern level, and (c) a comparison against alternatives (Reg BI). The **quantitative/pattern test** (a series of individually suitable trades can be excessive together) is the closest regulatory analogue to OrderGuard's sequence-level supervision.

### Cited Findings
- FINRA Rule 2111(a) profile fields: age, other investments, financial situation and needs, tax status, investment objectives, investment experience, time horizon, liquidity needs, risk tolerance, and any other information the customer discloses. — [FINRA Rule 2111](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/2111)
- Three obligations (SM .05): **reasonable-basis** (suitable for at least some investors, based on reasonable diligence and an understanding of risks and rewards); **customer-specific** (suitable for this customer's profile); **quantitative** (a series of transactions, each suitable alone, must not be excessive together, for a broker with actual or de facto control). "No single test defines excessive activity". Turnover rate, cost-equity ratio and in-and-out trading are factors. — [FINRA Rule 2111](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/2111)
- A firm may treat a profile factor as irrelevant only with a reasonable basis "documented with specificity" (SM .04). An explicit recommendation to **hold** is covered (SM .03). — [FINRA Rule 2111](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/2111)
- Institutional exemption 2111(b): customer-specific suitability is met if the firm reasonably believes the customer can evaluate risks independently and the customer affirmatively says it is exercising independent judgement. If decisions are delegated to an agent, these factors apply to the **agent**. — [FINRA Rule 2111](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/2111)
- Rule 2111 does not apply to recommendations covered by Reg BI (SEA Rule 15l-1) (SM .08). — [FINRA Rule 2111](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/2111)
- FINRA FAQ: documentation is risk-based, and complying does not necessarily turn on documenting the basis for the recommendation. — [FINRA 2111 FAQ](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/key-topics/suitability/faq)
- Reg BI Care Obligation (SEC staff bulletin, 2023, 20 Q&As): understand risks, rewards and **costs**. Consider **reasonably available alternatives** early in forming the recommendation, compared against the investor's profile. A limited menu alone is not a basis to conclude no alternatives exist. Firms need a reasonable process to scope alternatives. Documenting the alternatives analysis is not specifically required but may be needed as evidence. The staff views the same framework as useful for advisers' fiduciary duty of care. — [SEC staff bulletin, Care Obligations](https://www.sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/division-trading-markets/broker-dealers/staff-bulletin-standards-conduct-broker-dealers-investment-advisers-care-obligations); summaries: [Proskauer](https://proskauer.com/alert/sec-staff-issues-bulletin-on-the-care-obligations-of-advisers-and-broker-dealers-to-retail-investors), [Sidley](https://sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2023/5/sec-publishes-additional-interpretive-guidance-on-reg-bi-and-investment-adviser-standard-of-care)
- MiFID II suitability (ESMA Guidelines, final report 23 September 2022, ESMA35-43-3172) applies to investment advice and **portfolio management**. From 2 August 2022 firms must collect and respect clients' **sustainability preferences**. Collected preferences must be granular enough to match against product sustainability features (para 26). Firms need not explain products where a client has no sustainability preferences. ESMA dropped prescriptive guidance on combinations of preferences after feedback. — [ESMA final guidelines](https://www.esma.europa.eu/node/101543); [Simmons & Simmons](https://www.simmons-simmons.com/en/publications/cl8myuemd6aq30a56egzxfuca/esma-s-mifid-suitability-guidelines-and-sustainability-preferences); [Linklaters](https://financialregulation.linklaters.com/post/102hxu2/final-report-and-revised-esma-guidelines-published-on-certain-aspects-of-mifid-ii). Application date: reported as 3 October 2023 (sources differ). — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/int/legal-updates/esma-guidelines-on-suitability-updated-to-take-into-account-sustainability-factors-risks-and-preferences-of-the-clients)
- An ESMA Common Supervisory Action found firms progressing but with recurring weaknesses in capturing and applying client sustainability preferences. — [EY Luxembourg, CSA insights](https://www.ey.com/en_lu/insights/wealth-asset-management/mifid-2-sustainability-preferences-insights-from-esma-supervisory-review)

### Inferences
- These regimes give a ready **three-level target** for System One: (L1) is the instrument or action reasonable for *anyone* (product-level understanding and risk); (L2) does it fit *this owner's* typed profile and mandate; (L3) is the *sequence* of the agent's actions excessive or drifting even if each action passes L1 and L2. Reg BI adds (L4): was a cheaper or less risky **alternative** reasonably available that serves the same purpose? That makes the decision comparative, not absolute.
- The institutional exemption's agent clause means that once an owner delegates to an AI agent, regulators look at the **agent's** capability and independent judgement. That points to OrderGuard needing a capability profile of the agent, not just the owner.
- MiFID's "granular enough to match product features" requirement is effectively a typing requirement: preferences must be stored in the same vocabulary as instrument attributes.

### Gaps
- Whether FINRA's proposed amendments to quantitative suitability (Regulatory Notice 18-13; SR-FINRA-2020-007 effective 30 June 2020) removed the "control" element was not confirmed from the rule page. Check the rule history.
- I did not retrieve firm-level suitability *testing* methods (surveillance thresholds, sampling rates). FINRA gives no numeric excessive-trading thresholds; turnover and cost-equity benchmarks used in case law were not sourced.

## 6. Preference elicitation: questionnaires and weaknesses, goal-based, consequence/scenario elicitation, IRL and inverse optimisation

### Takeaway
Standard risk-tolerance questionnaires are weak measurement instruments. Klement's review found they explain **under 15%** of the variation in investors' risky-asset holdings, and advisers' heuristic classifications over- or under-estimate validated scores. Better approaches elicit preferences over **outcome distributions** (Distribution Builder) or **infer** risk preferences from observed choices (robo-advisor RL, inverse optimisation), with an explicit trade-off between asking the client again and acting on stale estimates.

### Cited Findings
- Klement (CFA Institute Research Foundation): standard questionnaire risk profiling is "highly unreliable" and typically explains less than 15% of the variation in risky-asset holdings. He attributes this to a focus on socioeconomic variables and hypothetical scenarios. — [CFA RF Brief, Investor Risk Profiling: An Overview](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/-/media/documents/article/rf-brief/rfbr-v1-n1-1-pdf.pdf); compilation: [Risk Profiling and Tolerance (2018)](https://www.cfainstitute.org/-/media/documents/book/rf-publication/2018/risk_compilation_2018.pdf)
- Behavioural biases affect both how clients perceive risk and how advisers interpret risk tolerance (Pompian brief). — [CFA RF Brief v2 n1](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/-/media/documents/article/rf-brief/rfbr-v2-n1-1-pdf.pdf)
- Hartnett, Gerrans & Faff (FAJ 2019): advisers should re-examine and resurvey clients whose standardised responses vary widely. They propose the standard deviation of standardised item responses as a per-client reliability measure and ask providers to publish score means and SDs. — [FAJ 2019](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/en/research/financial-analysts-journal/2019/0015198X-2019-1575160); [Bond University record](https://research.bond.edu.au/en/publications/trusting-clients-financial-risk-tolerance-survey-scores/)
- Subedar, McCrae & Gerace (EFMA 2007), using about 70,000 investor responses: advisers' heuristic risk categorisation is inefficient and in some cases grossly under- or over-estimates risk tolerance measured psychometrically. — [EFMA 2007 paper](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2007-Austria/papers/0354.pdf)
- Evidence conflicts on stability. Morningstar cites test-retest evidence that risk tolerance is relatively stable, while other commentary argues it is fickle. — [Morningstar, Measuring Client Risk Tolerance](https://www.morningstar.com/lp/measurement-client-risk-tolerance?con=22781); contradicted by [Sunday Times (PressReader)](https://www.pressreader.com/south-africa/sunday-times-1107/20171203/283682594963515). Neither underlying study was verified.
- Goldstein, Johnson & Sharpe (J. Consumer Research 2008), the **Distribution Builder**: users pick a desired distribution of future outcomes, constrained by market prices and the amount invested, instead of picking products. They tested reliability and predictive validity, estimated risk-aversion and loss-aversion parameters, and argue the tool helps people *construct* better preferences, not just reveal them. — [Stanford PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/~wfsharpe/retecon/GJSJCR08.pdf); [Columbia Business School](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/choosing-outcomes-versus-choosing-products-consumer-focused-retirement-investment)
- Alsabah, Capponi, Ruiz Lacedelli & Stern (J. Financial Econometrics 2021): a robo-advisor learns an investor's unknown risk aversion from her portfolio choices across market environments. It trades off *costly solicitations* of the investor's choices against *autonomous trading on stale estimates*. Its value converges to that of an omniscient advisor, and by correcting investor mistakes it can outperform the stand-alone investor. — [arXiv 1911.02067](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02067)
- Yu, Wang & Dong: **inverse optimisation** inside mean-variance, treating risk preference as a learnable parameter estimated in real time from observed portfolios. A related online inverse optimisation study infers both risk preference and expected returns from allocation history. — [arXiv 2010.01687](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.01687); [arXiv 2105.09264](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.09264)

### Inferences
- For System One's target, owner preferences should be stored as **parameters with uncertainty** (e.g. a risk-aversion posterior and a reliability score in the Hartnett style), not as a single questionnaire bucket. Low reliability should widen the "ask the owner" band.
- The Capponi solicitation-vs-staleness trade-off maps directly to OrderGuard's escalate-to-owner decision. Escalation has a cost, and acting on a stale purpose has a cost. That framing gives a principled threshold.
- Distribution-Builder-style elicitation (show the owner outcome distributions or consequences for a concrete action, then ask "acceptable?") is a natural way to collect labelled "serves / doesn't serve" data from owners. It also handles the finding that preferences are partly constructed during elicitation.
- Goal-based investing (separate mental-account sub-portfolios per goal) is relevant, but was not sourced in this pass. See Gaps.

### Gaps
- No source retrieved on goal-based investing frameworks (e.g. Das, Markowitz, Scheid & Statman 2010) or on regulatory acceptance of revealed-preference profiling under MiFID or Reg BI.
- I did not verify the Lucarelli, Brighetti & Uberti misclassification figures cited in commentary.

## 7. Expert disagreement about "serves the purpose" and measured inter-rater agreement

### Takeaway
I found **no study that directly measures inter-rater agreement on "does this trade serve this mandate"**. Proxy evidence suggests substantial disagreement wherever a qualitative label is turned into a measurement: ESG ratings correlate only 0.38–0.71 across providers (credit ratings correlate at about 0.99), about a third of funds self-designate mismatched benchmarks, managers read "80% equities" as 67–90%, and advisers' heuristic risk labels diverge from validated scores.

### Cited Findings
- Berg, Kölbel & Rigobon (Review of Finance 2022), six ESG raters: pairwise correlations range from **0.38 to 0.71**. Measurement divergence explains more than 50% of the disagreement, with scope and weight making up the rest. Secondary sources give different averages (0.54 and 0.61) and shares (measurement 56% and scope 38%, vs measurement 50.1% and weight 13.2%), so check the paper before quoting a single number. — [SSRN 3438533](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3438533); [MIT Sloan](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/node/19378); [IrisCarbon summary](https://iriscarbon.com/key-findings-from-a-study-on-the-divergence-of-esg-ratings)
- For comparison, Moody's and S&P credit ratings correlate at about 0.99. — [MIT Sloan](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/node/19378)
- Different managers code "minimum 80% in equities" as minimum 90% or minimum 67%. — [Acuity KP](https://www.acuitykp.com/blog/challenges-faced-in-coding-of-investment-restrictions)
- About one-third of active US equity funds self-designate a style-mismatched benchmark. — [Sensoy 2009](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:92:y:2009:i:1:p:25-39)
- Advisers' heuristic risk categorisations grossly under- or over-estimate psychometric scores in some cases. — [Subedar et al. 2007](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2007-Austria/papers/0354.pdf)
- FINRA states that "no single test defines excessive activity", so the regulator itself declines a crisp decision boundary. — [FINRA Rule 2111](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/2111)

### Inferences
- If expert raters were asked to label "serves the purpose" for agent actions, agreement would likely be high on hard-constraint cases and much lower on qualitative tilt cases. ESG-style measurement divergence (what counts as "overpriced", "a theme", "tech") would dominate. This is an inference; no direct measurement exists.
- Berg et al.'s decomposition gives a design rule. Most disagreement comes from **measurement**, not weighting. OrderGuard should fix the measurement definitions (which classification counts as "tech", which valuation metric counts as "overpriced", how a "theme" is defined) at delegation time and store them in the typed mandate. Then the remaining judgement is about the trade, not the vocabulary.
- For System One's evaluation set, OrderGuard should collect several labels per case and report agreement (e.g. Cohen's or Fleiss' kappa), split by case type (hard constraint, tilt, thesis consistency). The ceiling on achievable model accuracy is the human agreement on that split. This is a method recommendation; no finance-specific kappa benchmark was found.

### Gaps
- No published inter-rater reliability (kappa or ICC) study was found for: suitability determinations by compliance reviewers, investment committee votes, consultant style-drift verdicts, or arbitrator outcomes in suitability cases. This is the largest evidence gap for defining System One's label quality.
- Morningstar vs Lipper category disagreement rates were not retrieved.
